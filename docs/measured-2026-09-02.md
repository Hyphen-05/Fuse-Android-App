# What the capture session measured — 2026-09-02

An overnight run of the plan in [capture-session-plan.md](capture-session-plan.md). Everything here
is measured on Joe's own hardware, driven by a Pixel 11 Pro XL (or a Pixel 9 Pro XL where noted) and
filmed on a Pixel 9 Pro XL. Raw CSVs are in `tools/calibration/`; the video is in `captures/`, which
is gitignored — about 6.4GB across 8 files, and it exists nowhere else.

**The numbers below are the ones that fell out during capture, not the result of an analysis pass.**
That pass has since happened — read [analysis-2026-09-02.md](analysis-2026-09-02.md) for what the
data actually says, and prefer it wherever the two disagree. Three figures here are superseded: the
write-survival framing (there is a 15Hz ceiling, and below it nothing is lost), the 57/53ms latency
(crossing-frame, biased high by half a frame), and `sustained_load`'s 407k writes (offered, not
delivered). This file stays as the record of what was *captured* and under what conditions.

## The wire

### Roughly half of all writes never reach the strip, and it depends on rate

`CalibrationWireLog` logs a row per send and per `onCharacteristicWrite` ack, per device.

| run | writes/sec | sends | acks | acked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `full_ramp` | ~1 | 862 | 862 | **100%** |
| `dark_ramp` (macro) | ~1 | 186 | 184 | 99% |
| `rate_ramp`, 1 device | 2-100 | 1688 | 690 | 41% |
| `rate_ramp`, 2 devices | 2-100 | 3376 | 1426 | 42% |
| `capture_all`, 1 device | mixed | 2862 | 1398 | 49% |
| `capture_all`, 2 devices | mixed | 5724 | 2862 | 50% |

Every ack that came back carried status 0. Nothing fails — writes are **superseded in
`DeviceWriteManager`'s queue** by a later command of the same type byte before transmission. The
fraction that survives is a function of **how fast the sequence writes** and of nothing else.

This is the mechanism behind uneven visual timing at speed. Joe watched `spacing_staircase` and
reported that the red/blue alternation was not evenly spaced and that the two strips disagreed with
each other; that is the staircase climbing into the rate where superseding bites, and different
commands being dropped on each device. **It is app-side, not the strip**, and it was invisible to
every measurement before this one — the colour CSV logs the command *leaving the app*, which is
precisely the moment before the dropping happens.

### A second strip costs nothing

Same sequence, same way, back to back: 41% acked with one device, 42% with two, and **713 acks each**
across the pair against 690 for the single. The first device got marginally *more* through with
company, which is noise.

So there is no per-strip contention to model. `multiDeviceThroughputFactor` has nothing left to
describe, and the old 0.71 figure was a property of a slower driving phone, not of the protocol.

### Endurance

`sustained_load`, 15.07 minutes, both strips, Pixel driver: **407,282 writes**, median gap 2ms, p95
5ms, one 1798ms hiccup, no disconnects. The old `rate_ramp` "ceiling" of ~82Hz was the *request*
topping out at 100Hz, not the hardware — do not quote it as a throughput bound.

## Wire-to-light latency — now measured

Previously impossible from a strips-only recording, because aligning video to log time using the
strip subtracts out the delay being measured. Solved by making the **driving phone its own camera**
(`LatencyCameraProbe`): the app sends the command and the app sees the light, so both events sit on
one clock in one process.

    rise  black -> white   n=20   min 24   median 57   max 144 ms
    fall  white -> black   n=20   min 15   median 53   max 187 ms

Frame interval was 33ms, so that is the quantisation — but the spread is four times it, meaning what
is visible is real delivery jitter rather than the sampling grid. Consistent with the sd 36ms
measured 2026-08-16. **Rise and fall agree closely: the strip is not slower to light than to darken.**

The measurement is only valid because `SENSOR_INFO_TIMESTAMP_SOURCE` came back `realtime`, so frame
timestamps share a base with `elapsedRealtimeNanos`. On a device reporting `unknown_monotonic` the
comparison must use `uptimeNanos` instead; the probe records the source in the CSV header and stamps
every write on both clocks so the analysis can pick, rather than assume. Getting this wrong yields a
plausible number that is wrong by however long the device has been asleep.

Exposure time is the floor: a frame integrates over its whole exposure, so an edge is located to
within that window and no finer. Quote it alongside the result rather than folding it in.

## Deep dimming does not flicker

`pwm_probe` holds a duty cycle and logs per-frame mean luma at a pinned 5ms exposure. A carrier slow
enough to be *seen* has a period longer than 5ms and so scatters the per-frame numbers; a faster one
averages out inside the exposure and is imperceptible anyway.

| duty | frames | mean luma | sd | cv |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5% | 594 | 5.69 | 0.112 | 2.0% |
| 10% | 592 | 17.53 | 0.123 | 0.7% |
| 40% | 592 | 53.41 | 0.284 | 0.5% |
| 100% | 593 | 82.58 | 0.425 | 0.5% |

Every sd is a fraction of one 8-bit level, and the absolute sd is *smallest* at 5% — the opposite of
what a slow carrier would do. Together with the 2026-08-19 smear result at 15%, dimming is
flicker-free across the range. **That was the last open risk against headroom scaling.**

Those means are also a firmware-brightness response, and it is plainly not proportional: 5% gives 7%
of full luma, 10% gives 21%, 40% gives 65%. Gamma-encoded and not linearised — `full_ramp`'s 1-100%
sweep is the proper version.

## What was captured, and where

All in `tools/calibration/`, prefixed by condition where it matters (`onedev_`, `twodev_`, `macro_`,
`bright_`, `dark_`, `pwm_`, `fast_`).

| sequence | what it holds |
|---|---|
| `full_ramp` ×2 exposures | every byte 0-255 at 1s, descending check, every brightness 1-100% |
| `dark_ramp` ×3 | bytes 0-32 at 1.5s; one at P1 framing, one macro on the 18° telephoto |
| `brightness_ramp` | the 15-step curve |
| `colour_primaries` | R, G, B swept alone, then secondaries — per-channel response and crosstalk |
| `brightness_x_colour` | 5 brightness levels × 5 colours |
| `cct_sweep` | warm/cold across its range |
| `transition_probe` | hard jumps, to see whether the firmware steps or glides |
| `write_type_probe` | plain (0x10) vs music (0x20) colour commands: filmed, bursted, interleaved |
| `spacing_staircase` | 11 stair levels 2-60ms |
| `rate_ramp` | 1-device and 2-device |
| `sustained_load` | 15 minutes |
| `capture_all` | all of the above in one 10-minute pass, 1-device and 2-device |
| `latency_camera` | in-app latency, two runs |
| `pwm_probe` | 5, 10, 40, 100% |

Exposure conventions used: **bright** = ISO 23-31, 1/40s (protects the top of the range from
clipping); **dark** = ISO 339-560, 1/40s (for bytes 0-32). No single exposure holds both ends — that
is the sensor's dynamic range, not a fixable workflow problem, which is why `full_ramp` was shot
twice.

## Traps found the hard way

**Never let the driver's screen sleep during a run.** The display turning off takes the app out of
TOP; Android then refuses `startForegroundService()` outright, the process is throttled, and
`delay()` inside a sequence stops advancing. On 2026-09-02 that silently stalled a `brightness_ramp`
and a `spacing_staircase` back to back — no CSV, no error, and ten minutes of video of a strip that
had stopped moving. The overlay now holds the screen awake for **every** sequence and pins brightness
to minimum for all but `latency_pulse`, so a dark room does not require a dark screen.

**A strip can report CONNECTED, ack every write with status 0, and emit nothing.** Seen 2026-09-02:
1688 sends, 690 acks, all successful, camera showing black. It is a device-side state and nothing
sent over BLE clears it — it needs a power cycle at the mains. Nothing in the app distinguishes this
from working; only looking at the strip does.

**`getCurrentlyControlledDeviceAddresses()` filters on active control, never on connection state.**
Disconnecting a device in the UI does not stop calibration writes being queued for it. To take a
device out of a run, turn **Active Control** off, not just Disconnect.

**Device names are the opposite way round to what they suggest.** `Fireworks`
(`11:66:F0:00:01:3D`) is the wall starburst with the long vertical strand. `RGB Bars` /
`Ambiance Bars` (`BE:16:0A:00:3B:DA`) is the pair of bars by the window, one device across both.

**Lock auto-rotate before driving a camera app by taps.** Picking the phone up rotates the UI and
every stored coordinate misses silently — a whole burst of shutter taps produced no photos.
