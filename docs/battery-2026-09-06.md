# The smoothness programme has a measured answer, and it is no

Blocks 0-3 of the test battery, run by Joe on 2026-09-06 at 25% brightness. Raw data in
`tools/perception/results/lab/`.

**Joe's own report is the headline and it came before the analysis:** block 2 was hard *"because
none of them were smooth"*, and block 3 *"because dithering always makes a flickery mess"*. Both
are borne out, and together they close the line of work that has been running since the 2026-09-04
steppiness report.

## What each block said

**Block 0 — grid.** Changes at bytes 8, 12, 16. Spacing 4, as `100 / 25` predicts. Controls clean.
The rule holds on this evening's setting; nothing new, which is what a re-baseline is for.

**Block 1 — the perceptual scale. This is the one that matters.** Smallest visible change, measured
at 100% firmware brightness where commanded byte and emitted level are the same number:

| emitted level | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| smallest visible change | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | **4** | **8** |

Monotone, no inconsistent answers, controls clean. Above level 32 it is close to Weber — 4/64 and
8/128 are both 6.3%. **Below level 48 the threshold is one output level, which is the smallest step
the hardware can make.**

**Block 2 — update rate. Uninformative, by a flaw in my design.** He picked the faster interval in
3 of 15 trials and said "can't tell" in 10. Read naively that says 10Hz is as good as 60Hz. What it
actually says is what he said out loud: none of them were smooth, so he was being asked to rank two
things that were both bad. See below — the block could not have worked.

*(Control caveat: 1 of 3 catch trials was called, a 33% false-positive rate. With n=3 that is one
answer and not a verdict, but this block's numbers are weak on both counts.)*

**Block 3 — dithering. Dead.** The dithered interval was picked out as flickering **8 times out of
8**, at every alternation period from 20ms to 100ms. And it did not even buy the level it was
supposed to: the lands-between comparisons went 4 correct out of 8 with 4 "can't tell", which is
chance. So dithering both fails to produce an intermediate level *and* visibly flickers.

## What they add up to

**The strip has roughly one output level per visible step across Joe's entire working range.**

At 25% he reaches emitted levels 1-64. Integrating block 1's thresholds over that range gives about
**46 visible steps in 64 levels — 1.4 levels per step.** Every output level the strip can make down
there is individually visible to him. There is no slack.

Two consequences, and they explain both of his complaints exactly:

- **More frames cannot help.** A dark fade from level 2 to level 22 crosses 21 output levels and
  contains about 20 visible steps. At 15Hz a 1.5s fade already has 22 updates, so every level is
  already being sent. At 60Hz the extra 68 updates are duplicates of levels already shown. **Block
  2 was asking about a variable that had no room to matter** — the fade's steppiness was set by the
  level count, which was identical in both intervals.
- **Dithering cannot help either.** It is the one mechanism that could synthesise a level between
  two others, and it is measured out: 8/8 detected as flicker. The reason is amplitude, not rate.
  Down where the levels are wanted, one level is a large fraction of the light, so the alternation
  is a deep modulation — and deep modulation is visible far below the flicker-fusion rate the link
  could reach anyway.

**So a smooth dark fade is not achievable on this hardware.** Not with faster writes, not with
dithering, not with a different brightness setting. The steps are visible, there is nothing between
them, and nothing can be put between them.

## Where smoothness does live

The same table says where it is: **above emitted level ~64, where several output levels fit inside
one visible step.** Across the full 1-255 range there are about 73 visible steps in 255 levels —
3.5 levels per step — because most of that range is bright.

A fade at levels 100-160 crosses 60 output levels and about **8 visible steps**. The same-sized fade
at levels 2-22 crosses 21 levels and about **20 visible steps**. That is the whole difference
between "smooth" and "steppy", and it is a fact about light and eyes rather than about the app.

**At 25%, Joe's maximum is level 64 — he is running the strip entirely inside the steppy region.**
Reaching the smooth region means more light, and that is a trade, not a fix: smoothness and
brightness are the same knob.

**This does not resurrect the retracted deep-dimming claim.** For a *fixed appearance* the reachable
lights are the same at any brightness setting, so low bytes at high brightness still buys nothing.
What is new is the opposite direction: to be smooth you have to be brighter, full stop.

## What this changes

**The smoothness programme is finished, with a negative result.** "Dark scenes are steppy" is not a
defect to be engineered away; it is what this strip does, for anyone. Three levers were proposed on
2026-09-06 and two of them are now closed by measurement:

- ~~Frame rate~~ — no new levels, so no new smoothness. **Still worth having for latency**, which is
  a separate question this battery has not asked.
- ~~Dithering~~ — measured dead, in both halves.
- **Compute in emitted levels** — survives, and is now clearly worth doing: the app currently smooths
  in a space where three of four values are aliases, which does not make things smoother but does
  waste writes and make the app's own reasoning wrong.

The live question is no longer "how do we make it smooth" but **"what should it do instead"**, which
is taste and belongs in blocks 4-6:

- Should Ambiance lift dark content into the smoother region, accepting that dark scenes stop being
  dark? That is the only remaining way to get a smooth dark scene, and its cost is exactly the thing
  it is trading for. **A taste question, and Joe's alone.**
- Where should the floor sit, given that everything below level ~5 is a handful of large steps?
- On a cut, is an eased transition through visible steps better or worse than an instant jump? If
  every intermediate level is visible anyway, easing may simply be a slower staircase.

## Caveats

- **Block 1 has one observation per cell.** The pattern is monotone with no inconsistencies, which
  is reassuring, but the thresholds are single crossings. Worth one repeat before anything expensive
  rests on the exact numbers.
- **The thresholds at levels 4-32 are floored by the instrument.** One output level is the smallest
  change the hardware can make, so "threshold 1" means "one level or less". The true figure could be
  a fraction of a level, which would mean each step is not merely noticeable but obvious — matching
  what he said after the first sitting, that changes were either clearly visible or absent with no
  "just about" in between. It cannot be measured directly, because the only way to make a sub-level
  change is dithering, and dithering is what block 3 just ruled out.
- **Block 1 ran at 100% brightness**, so his adaptation state was not the one he normally views in.
  The cross-check passed: levels 4-32 came back at threshold 1, which agrees with the probe's
  finding that every level from 2 to 17 is visible at 25%.
- **Block 2 should be rebuilt as a latency question**, not a smoothness one. As designed it cannot
  distinguish its own rates.
