# Blocks 4-6: one answer, one hint, and one block that never asked its question

Run by Joe on the evening of 2026-09-06 at 25% firmware brightness, floor at byte 5. Raw data in
`tools/perception/results/lab/`.

Read the health numbers before the votes. Two of these three blocks fail their own controls, and the
third was played at the wrong brightness by a bug in how it was built.

## Block 4 — settling speed. **This one produced a result.**

Catch trials clean (0 of 2 false positives). 9 of 18 comparisons answered "can't tell", and **the
unsures are not spread — they are exactly the adjacent pairs**:

| pair | settles in | answered |
|---|---|---|
| 25 vs 50ms | ~175 vs ~350ms | **can't tell, 3 of 3** |
| 120 vs 300ms | ~840 vs ~2100ms | **can't tell, 3 of 3** |
| every fast-vs-slow pair | — | answered 9 of 9 |

So there is one boundary he can see, it sits between ~50ms and ~120ms, and within each side the arms
are indistinguishable. Across that boundary, **the faster arm won 7 of 9**.

Split by anchor — which is why the block asks at two — that 7 of 9 is not evenly earned:

| anchor | jump | fast-vs-slow trials | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| level 2 → 10 (dark) | 8 visible steps | **faster won 5 of 5** | unanimous, repeats agree |
| level 42 → 62 (bright) | 8 visible steps | faster won 2 of 4 | a coin; both hard flips live here |

**In the dark he wants it fast and says so consistently. When it is bright he has no preference.**
That is mechanically sensible: down at level 2 every output level is a visible step, so a slow ease
is a staircase crawling one visible rung at a time; up at 42-62 several levels fit inside one step,
so the ease is smooth at any speed and only its duration differs.

### What this says about the shipped number

`AmbianceOutputInterpolator.HALF_LIFE_MS` is 50, which sits on the fast side of the only boundary he
can see, and he cannot tell it from 25. **There is no evidence the shipped value should change.**

It also settles the 2026-09-04 argument on the wall rather than by revert: that change made the
ease ~1.8x slower, into the region he rejects 5 of 5 — **at the dark anchor, which is exactly where
he reported the problem**. The revert was right, and now for a measured reason rather than a
procedural one.

### Caveats

One sitting, and the load-bearing number is 5 decisive trials at one anchor. Worth one repeat before
anything is built on it. The arms were also badly spaced: two clusters of two, when four arms spread
across the 50-120ms boundary would have located it instead of merely bracketing it.

## Block 5 — cut or ease. **Suggestive, not established.**

**Controls fail: 2 of 3 catch trials were answered decisively**, against a 20% limit, so this is
reported and not concluded from.

Ease won 8, cut won 2, 5 can't tell — and it is orderly by size:

| jump | cut | ease | can't tell |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 visible steps | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 4 steps | 0 | 2 | 1 |
| 8 steps | 0 | 2 | 1 |
| 16 steps | 0 | 3 | 0 |
| 33 steps | 1 | 0 | 2 |

The block was designed to find the size **above which a cut beats an ease**. Within the range his
brightness can reach, there isn't one: ease is preferred from 4 visible steps up, and the largest
jump goes indecisive rather than flipping. If that holds up, easing everything — what the app already
does — is right, and the open question is only whether the very smallest changes are worth easing at
all.

**Amended 2026-09-07: the catch rate is not the objection it looked like.** See the section at the
end — six of six decisive catch answers across all four runs named the second interval, while real
comparisons tracked the arm across an order swap six times out of seven. He breaks ties by saying
"the second one" rather than "can't tell", which is not guessing and not inattention. So this block
is limited by its **n and its unsure rate**, not by a failed control. Its direction stands and a
re-run with mirrored repeats would settle it cheaply.

## Block 6 — dark scenes. **Spoiled by a bug of mine. Do not read the numbers.**

The block builds its stimuli in a 100%-brightness context, where a commanded byte and an emitted
level are the same number. `LabBatteryScreen` drives firmware brightness **per trial**, from
`LabTrial.brightnessPercent` — and the helper that builds preference trials left that field null.
So the block played at Joe's own 25%, dividing every emitted level by four:

| arm, as designed | commanded | actually emitted |
|---|---|---|
| lift top level 96 (the smooth region) | byte 96 | **level 24** |
| lift top level 44 | byte 44 | level 11 |
| lift top level 20 (as-is) | byte 20 | level 5 |
| floor at level 10 | byte 10 | level 3 |
| floor at level 5 | byte 5 | level 2 |

**The lift question was never asked.** Its whole point is a comparison against the region above
level ~64 where several output levels fit inside one visible step, and the brightest arm arrived at
24 — still deep inside the steppy region. The three arms were a dim scene against a slightly less
dim scene against a slightly less dim scene again.

The floor question was asked, but at a quarter of its intended levels: floors of 0/1/2/3 rather than
0/2/5/10. Its answers (0 wins 4, 10 wins 3, 2 wins 1, 5 wins 0) are messy, its catch rate fails, and
they are about a different question from the one on the card.

### Fixed and re-run — see below

## Two changes to how these are read

Both were found by this data rather than reasoned into existence.

- **Pairs are keyed by their scope, not just by their arms.** The same two half-lives asked at a
  dark anchor and at a bright one were collating into one "repeated pair", so a preference that
  simply depends on level was being counted as self-contradiction. `preferenceReadingsBy` splits a
  reading by any meta key.
- **A hard flip is not the same as an unsure.** `consistency` counted "can't tell once, picked A the
  other time" as a disagreement. Block 4 scored 0.5 on it and looked like a failed block; the actual
  content was one soft case and two hard flips, both at the anchor where he had no preference to be
  consistent about. `trustworthy` now gates on `hardFlipRate` instead, because a block where he was
  often unsure is a block whose arms were too close — which is already reported, as `unsureRate`.

# Block 6, re-run 2026-09-07: the lift does not win

`near_black_1788765803145.json`. Every trial pinned at 100% firmware brightness this time, so the
question was actually put. `preferencePair` and `catchPair` now take the context and pin
`brightnessPercent` from it; `LabBlocksTest` asserts both directions.

## The stimulus did what it was designed to do

Worth establishing before reading the answer, because the first run's failure was exactly this going
unchecked. Scaling the scene's shape to a higher top level preserves its ratios — so each leg looks
the same size — while multiplying the hardware rungs available to render it:

| top level | scene levels | rungs per leg | rungs per unit of ratio-change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 (as-is) | 4, 11, 7, 20, 9, 14, 5 | 7, 4, 13, 11, 5, 9 | ~7-14 |
| 44 | 9, 24, 15, 44, 20, 31, 11 | 15, 9, 29, 24, 11, 20 | ~15-30 |
| 96 (smooth region) | 19, 53, 34, 96, 43, 67, 24 | 34, 19, 62, 53, 24, 43 | ~33-66 |

**Five times the rungs for the same apparent movement.** If lifting buys smoothness, this stimulus
had it to sell.

## The answer

| pair | answers |
|---|---|
| 20 vs 44 | can't tell, can't tell |
| 20 vs 96 | **20, then 96 — same presentation order both times** |
| 44 vs 96 | can't tell, then 96 |

Three of six comparisons were "can't tell", and the one pair with a large difference was answered
**both ways round under an identical presentation**. That is the strongest available evidence of no
preference: not a close call, but the same question producing opposite answers.

**So the lift loses, and the smoothness programme is now closed at both ends.** Blocks 0-3 said a
smooth dark fade cannot be built on this hardware. The only route left was to stop making the scene
dark, and that is a taste question, and the taste says no. Nothing remains to try.

It is also consistent with what he said from the wall on 2026-09-04, when a brightness correction was
proposed and rejected: dark scenes do not read as wrong to him. The lab has now asked the same thing
the other way round — would you rather they were brighter and smoother — and got the same answer.

**Caveat:** six lift trials, three of them "can't tell". This could only have detected a strong
preference. It rules out the lift being an obvious win; it does not prove indifference.

## The floor question is not on one axis

Votes came out 0:3, 5:2, 10:2, 2:1 with a cycle in them — 0 beats 5, 5 beats 10, 10 beats 0. A cycle
is not a bad answer, it means the arms differ in more than one way that matters. Plausibly "goes
fully dark" is a different *kind* of ending from "holds a glow", and ranking them on one line was the
wrong question. **No floor recommendation comes out of this.**

## The catch trials were measuring the wrong thing, and the fix is better than they were

Across four taste-block runs, **six of six decisive answers on catch trials named the second
interval** — a catch shows two *identical* intervals, so the honest answer is "can't tell".

But on comparisons with a real difference, when the same pair was asked in both orders **he named
the same arm six times out of seven**.

Put together: he does not guess, and he was not inattentive. **He breaks ties by saying "the second
one" instead of "can't tell."** A catch trial cannot tell that apart from carelessness, and the two
call for opposite responses — so blocks 5 and 6 should not have been discounted on their catch rate.

The order flip can tell them apart, and it is now the block's credibility number:

- names the **same arm** across a swap → a real preference
- names the **same interval letter** → an order effect
- names neither → genuine inconsistency

Every consistency repeat is now a mirror of its original rather than a fresh randomisation — the
first version left about half of them in the same order, controlling for nothing — and
`PreferenceReading.orderRobustness` gates `trustworthy` at 0.75, which is where his real answers
already sit.

**One consequence worth carrying:** because ties are broken rather than declared, `unsureRate`
*understates* how often he could not tell. Read a decisive answer on a hard pair as weaker than it
looks.
