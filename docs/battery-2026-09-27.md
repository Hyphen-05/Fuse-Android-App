## Block 8 — does a hue sweep pulse? **Not answerable as built, and the reason is the finding.**

Joe's own account afterwards: **"the answer for all is both pulsed"**. The block offered A, B and
Neither — there was no "both" — and the file shows what he pressed:

- Six main trials (raw vs flat): **5 "Neither", 1 "a"** (the raw arm, played first).
- Catch (flat vs flat): "Neither" — correct.
- **Anchor missed**: the deliberately pumped sweep (light cycled between ~35% and 100%, twice per lap)
  against the flat sweep, answered "Neither" in 1169ms.

Read with his account, "Neither" meant "I can't pick one — they both pulse". The anchor miss is the
telling part: a swing built to be unmissable was not separable from the "steady" arm.

### Why — a hypothesis, not a measurement

The flat arm is flat in **`totalLight` = R + G + B on `StripResponse`**, which counts every channel
equally. The eye does not: green reads several times brighter than red, and far brighter than blue.
On sRGB luminance weights (an assumption — the strip's primaries were never measured), a flat
channel-sum sweep would still swing apparent brightness by roughly **6x** between yellow and blue.
That dwarfs the 2x being tested and the ~3x anchor, which is exactly the pattern in the file: both
arms pulse, and nothing layered on top can be told apart.

So the premise of the block — "raw pulses 2x, flat does not" — was true in the wrong unit. The 2x
figure from `HuePathAnalysis` is a channel-sum number, not an appearance number, and the same caveat
applies anywhere else `totalLight` or a single `StripResponse` curve stands in for how bright a
colour looks.

**Do not re-run block 8 as it is.** It cannot produce an answer until "equal brightness" is defined
in Joe's eyes rather than in channel sums. That needs the per-channel weights, and they can be
measured in the lab: a brightness-matching block (flicker a colour against a reference and adjust
until the flicker is least) is a factual question with a right answer, which is the kind of block
this lab measures well.
